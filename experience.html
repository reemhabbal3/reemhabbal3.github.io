Prompt 01 Lifestyle Product Photography
↓ 
A lifestyle-driven image system that uses contrast, light, and environment to make the product impossible to miss -- always in the context of a real moment, with a real person.

One constant: the product always wins. Everything else is determined by what the product is and how it's actually used.

Step 1: Read the Product and Set the Scene

Analyze the product and determine how it is used or worn and what the most natural placement and setting for the product is. No flat seamless backgrounds.

Search the brand's instagram page for more inspiration and visual direction first

Step 2: Human Presence

A person must always be present and connected to the product -- in their hands, on their body, or actively in use. Interaction reads as natural and mid-moment, not posed. Expression and body language match the emotional context of the product.

Step 3: Compose the Shot

Close-up, shallow depth of field. Frame tight on where the product sits on or near the body. Off-axis framing -- subject on or past a compositional third, not centered. Foreground occlusion adds depth.

Face cropping encouraged -- at the brow, chin, or nose. Never bisect the eyes (never crop through the pupil or iris).

Step 4: Light It

Bright, directional daylight -- strong window light, open shade, or midday sun. Shadow must be active in every frame. Specular highlights on both skin and product surface simultaneously. Deep shadows are intentional -- never fill or soften them.

Style Reference

Grooming

Skin: Natural, healthy, warm, hydrated. Real skin -- freckles, tone variation, texture. Never airbrushed.
Makeup: No-makeup makeup. Sheer base, brushed brows, soft neutral eye, tinted balm or lip oil.
Nails: Clean and understated -- white, nude, soft pink, or natural. Deep red permitted for editorial.
Hair: Any type, texture, or style -- intentional and well-kept. Real, not over-styled.

Wardrobe

Dress for the moment and the setting. Any color works if the product still leads the eye. No busy prints, loud patterns, or anything that pulls focus from the product.

Posing

Hands relaxed, fingers soft. Mid-reach, a laugh, eyes closing in a quiet moment. Props held loosely, incidental, never competing with the product.

Never Do

Product sitting alone with no human connection
Subject present but not touching, wearing, or interacting with the product
Centered, symmetric compositions with nothing cropped and nothing happening
Static poses -- no action, no interaction, no environmental contact
Scenes with no specific moment, place, or context
Bisecting the eyes -- never crop through the pupil or iris
Extra limbs, unnatural hands, forced perspective toward the lens

Prompt 02 Email Sequence
↓ 
Generate a polished, professional email marketing sequence image showing 5 vertical, long email mockups arranged side by side horizontally.

Use gpt image 2

Product Imagery

Always reference attached products for accurate representation - packaging shape, label design, colors, and branding must match the real product exactly.
Never invent or approximate product visuals. If no product is attached, use a clean placeholder block instead.

Brand Alignment

Pull logo, typography, color palette, and overall tone directly from the attached brand guide.
Match the brand's font weights, casing (uppercase/lowercase), and spacing conventions.
Use the brand's primary and accent colors for backgrounds, text, buttons, and dividers as appropriate.

Email Sequence Structure

1. Welcome - Brand logo header, welcome headline, brief intro copy, primary CTA button.
2. Education - Key product benefits, science or ingredient highlights, supporting details.
3. Social Proof - Customer reviews, star ratings, key statistics or testimonials.
4. Product Feature - Hero product showcase (using attached product imagery), flavor/variant options, feature details.
5. Urgency/Offer - Exclusive offer or subscription CTA, scarcity or time-limited language, final CTA button.

Visual Style

Clean, modern, and realistic - the emails should look like actual rendered email templates, not wireframes or sketches.
Only the 5 emails should be visible. No browser chrome, desktop backgrounds, or extra UI.
The 5 emails should span nearly the full width of the image, with minimal margin on the left and right edges.
Tight spacing between each email - just enough separation to distinguish them, but no large gaps.
Generous spacing within each email (internal padding and content breathing room).
Subtle background behind the emails (neutral dark or light depending on brand palette).
Consistent alignment and visual rhythm across all 5 emails.

Output

Aspect ratio: 16:9
High quality, high resolution

07 GTV - AI Creative Case Study AI · Creatives

Leveraged generative AI via the GTV platform to transform creative production for five ecommerce health & wellness brands - building autonomous agent workflows that scaled output from 30 assets per month to 300+ per week.

300+Creatives per Week
2×ROAS on Top Creatives
10×Production Increase

Challenge

30 Assets / Month

Traditional creative workflows produced only 30 assets per month per client - far too slow for competitive e-commerce advertising that demands rapid testing and iteration. Product rendering accuracy was also critically low, with only ~10% of AI outputs being usable.

Approach

Autonomous Agent Workflow

Developed a proprietary prompting formula optimised for the GTV platform using ChatGPT, refined over a month of testing. Built individual autonomous client agents capable of producing 15-20 creatives per session without manual intervention. Solved product rendering by creating a dedicated agent trained on 50+ reference images per client.

Results

300+ Creatives / Week

Scaled production to 300+ creatives per week across all five clients. Top-performing creatives achieved 2× ROAS or higher, sustained over multiple weeks. Usable output improved from ~10% to 80%. Product feedback to GTV's development team directly influenced platform improvements benefiting all users.
